Build something · Prompt collection

Prompts for cinematic AI visuals

Give a scene a point of view. Describe what happens, where the camera sits and what the light is doing before adding more adjectives.

By Sarah Forrest · Updated November 22, 2026

Download the PDF ↓
What you’ll leave with

Two structured scene briefs you can adapt, plus a repeatable way to refine them.

Start with a visual intention

Choose one subject, one action and one camera movement. Separate the elements you need from the elements you are willing to explore. These examples organize a creative brief as JSON; they are not a universal API format and have not been validated across video models. Paste them as reference text or adapt them to your tool's supported controls.

Scene 01 / A quiet train journey

{
  "scene": "A passenger beside a train window, holding a coffee as the landscape passes",
  "action": "Steam rises while the passenger watches the view",
  "camera": "Steady medium close-up, gentle push toward the window",
  "light": "Soft morning daylight, neutral whites, cool shadows",
  "palette": "Muted blue, charcoal and natural skin tones",
  "sound_brief": "Quiet carriage ambience and the rhythm of the train",
  "avoid": ["yellow color cast", "invented text", "abrupt camera movement"]
}

Scene 02 / An everyday ritual

{
  "scene": "A heat-safe transparent kettle on a kitchen stove",
  "action": "Small bubbles rise as water heats and steam gathers",
  "camera": "Static close-up at counter height",
  "light": "Window daylight with soft reflections on glass",
  "palette": "Cool gray, clear glass and soft blue",
  "sound_brief": "A quiet kitchen and gently heating water",
  "avoid": ["yellow filter", "extra objects appearing", "warped kettle geometry"]
}

Refine one variable at a time

Keep the subject and action fixed while you compare camera movement or light. Save the prompt with the result so you can see which change helped. Sound instructions only apply when your tool supports audio; otherwise use them as an editing brief.

Review before sharing

For edits to your own photographs, distinguish color adjustments from generative changes. A prompt alone cannot guarantee that a person's face will stay pixel-identical.

  • Does the action remain consistent from start to finish?
  • Are hands, faces, objects and reflections stable?
  • Is any text or branding inaccurate?
  • Do you have permission to use supplied images or likenesses?
  • Would a viewer mistake a generated scene for documentary footage? Label it clearly when that distinction matters.

Start here · Practical guide

Build an assistant for one recurring task

Start with something you already do every week. Give your assistant a clear job, useful context and a definition of a good answer.

By Sarah Forrest · Updated November 22, 2026

A reusable prompt for turning rough notes into a weekly update.

01 / Choose a small job

A useful assistant starts with a specific task: turn meeting notes into an update, explain an unfamiliar concept or review a first draft. Choose one input and one output. A saved prompt can be enough; it does not need tools or permission to act on your behalf.

  • Who will use the answer?
  • What information will you provide each time?
  • What should the assistant produce?
  • What still needs your judgment?

02 / Write the brief

Use the template below in your preferred AI chat. Replace the bracketed fields, then paste a short set of notes. Use information you are permitted to share.

Help me turn my weekly notes into a concise project update.
Audience: [team or reader].
Use only the notes I provide. Do not invent progress, names, dates or decisions. If something important is missing, mark it as unknown and ask a question.
Write in plain language. Keep the update under 200 words.
Return: 1. Completed work 2. Next steps 3. Blockers 4. Questions to resolve.
Keep proposed actions separate from confirmed decisions. Do not send or publish anything.
My notes: [paste notes here]

03 / Show what good looks like

Examples make your expectations concrete. If your note says 'Draft ready; waiting on Maya; no launch date', a useful summary is 'The draft is ready. Maya's review is pending. Launch date is not confirmed.' It should not become 'We are launching next week'. Add one example that demonstrates the distinction you care about.

04 / Test before you reuse it

Try three inputs: clear notes, incomplete notes and contradictory notes. Compare the result with the original. Save the prompt only when it reliably follows the brief.

  • Are all names, dates and claims supported?
  • Are unknowns visible?
  • Are the sections useful to the reader?
  • Did it preserve the distinction between an idea and a decision?

Make it yours

Change one instruction at a time. If answers are vague, ask for evidence from the notes. If they are too long, shorten the requested format. Save the prompt, a good example and a reminder of what you still review. That is your first reusable assistant.

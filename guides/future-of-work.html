Understand what’s changing · Reading notes

Three readings on the future of work

What is changing about work, and what can we practice now? Read across different kinds of evidence before turning a headline into a career decision.

By Sarah Forrest · Updated November 22, 2026

Download the PDF ↓
What you’ll leave with

Three starting points and a worksheet for connecting research to your own work.

01 / Look at early-career pathways

The World Economic Forum's June 2026 paper on AI and entry-level work offers a framework for thinking about access, job design and learning pathways. Read it with this question: if routine tasks change, where will beginners get the practice that builds judgment? It is a framework for action, not a forecast of your individual job.

02 / Read employer expectations carefully

The Future of Jobs Report 2025 draws on employer expectations about work and skills through 2030. Use it to identify questions worth investigating in your industry. Survey expectations are not guaranteed outcomes, and the report covers multiple economic and technological changes, not AI alone.

03 / Compare with labor-market signals

LinkedIn's research collection provides reports on hiring, skills and economic opportunity. Choose a report for your location and period, and check its methodology. Platform activity can reveal useful patterns, but it is not a complete census of all workers or jobs. Keep the date with any statistic you save.

Turn reading into a small experiment

My working question: how do we redesign work so people can build capability while using new tools? Technology adoption also involves learning, incentives and the way teams organize their work.

  • Choose one task you do each week.
  • List the knowledge, judgment and coordination it requires.
  • Identify one part AI might assist with.
  • Decide what quality looks like before you try it.
  • Compare the result with your usual approach, including review time.
  • Write down what you still need to learn.
